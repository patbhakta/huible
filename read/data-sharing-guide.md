# Sharing What Brings Them Back

> Some of the most precious things you have are the everyday ones — a quick text, a voicemail you never deleted, a photo where they're not ready. You don't have to share any of it. But if you'd like to, this will help you know what tends to matter most, and why.
>
> There are no requirements here. No minimums. No "right" file types. Whatever you have, in whatever form you have it, is welcome. We'll handle the rest.

---

## A Word Before You Begin

Looking through old messages and photos can bring up a lot. You might laugh. You might cry. You might find something you'd forgotten and need to sit with it for a while.

That's okay. That's the whole point.

Take your time. Step away when you need to. Come back tomorrow, or next week, or never — every single thing you share is a gift, and nothing is owed.

A few things that may help:

- **You don't have to organize anything.** Forward an email, drop in a screenshot, paste a memory from your notes app. However it arrives is fine.
- **Duplicates are completely fine.** If you're not sure whether you already sent something, send it again. We'd rather have too much than have you second-guessing yourself.
- **If something feels too private to share,** trust that feeling. Keep it. We don't need everything — we need what you're comfortable giving.
- **If you're not sure where to find something,** just ask. We can walk you through it, gently.

---

## Their Words (The Heart of It All)

This is the part that matters most — the everyday words they actually used. Not the big speeches, but the small ones.

The "did you eat yet?" texts. The way they signed off every call. The running joke that lasted twenty years. The good-morning message they sent every single day. The advice they gave you, in their words, when you needed it most.

**That's where their real voice lives.** In the small moments, not the big ones.

If you have any of these, they help more than anything else:

- Old text messages or chat threads you've kept
- Letters, cards, or notes they wrote — even quick ones
- Emails from them, or forwarded to you
- A journal, a blog, or anything they wrote down
- Stories you've saved about things they said

**If you're wondering what kind:** whatever you have. Screenshots, exported chats, copy-pasted text, photos of handwritten notes. We'll take it from there.

**If you're wondering how much:** whatever feels right. A handful of messages can be enough. So can years of archives. There's no number we're counting toward.

**If you only share one thing,** let it be the words. Everything else is bonus.

---

## Their Voice (Only If You're Ready)

We know this one can be hard.

If you have any recordings of their voice — a voicemail, a birthday message, a video where they're just talking, even a few seconds of them saying "hey" — that helps us understand how they actually sounded. How they paused. How they laughed. The music of the way they spoke.

**But hearing their voice might bring up a lot right now.** That's real, and there's no rush on this. Some people are ready. Some people aren't, and may never be. Both are completely fine.

You can:

- Share voice now, if you're ready
- Add it later, whenever that is — weeks, months, years
- Never share it at all, and we'll still build something beautiful from words alone

If you have something and you'd like to share it:

- A voicemail you saved
- A voice note from a messaging app
- A clip from a video where they're speaking
- Anything, really — however short, however rough

**If they didn't leave any recordings,** that's okay. Many people don't. Words alone carry more than enough of who they were.

---

## Photos (The Ones That Feel Like Them)

Photos help us see them — not just what they looked like, but who they were.

The candid ones are gold. Him laughing at dinner. Her caught mid-eye-roll. The two of you being silly. Real life, not posed. Those wedding portraits are beautiful, but they don't capture the way he'd make a face when she said something ridiculous. The blurry ones from a holiday. The one where nobody was ready. The one where everyone was.

**What helps most:**

- Photos where they're being themselves
- Photos of them with the people they loved
- Photos from the moments that mattered — birthdays, trips, ordinary Tuesdays

**How many:** however many feels right. A few is plenty. If you have more you'd like to share, wonderful.

**If you're wondering whether a photo is "good enough":** if it makes you feel something when you look at it, it's the right one.

---

## Video (The Most Personal, The Most Optional)

Video captures things nothing else can. The way they moved. Their gestures. The look on their face when they were happy. The way they tilted their head when they were thinking.

A clip of them being themselves — even ten seconds — can hold an entire person.

But video is the most personal thing you can share, and the most optional. **Only if and when you're ready.** Some people aren't, and that is completely fine. This is a door, not a requirement.

If you do have something:

- A home video, even a short one
- A clip someone took at a gathering
- A recording of a moment that mattered

**If you don't have any,** or don't want to share — that's completely okay. Nothing is lost. Words, voice, and photos already carry so much.

---

## Their World Online (If It Exists, If You Want)

Some people lived partly online — funny updates, photos, comments to friends, the little personality that came through in posts. If they did, that can be a treasure trove of who they were.

**This is always your choice, and always your data.** We never scrape, we never ask for your passwords, and we never look at anything you don't hand us yourself.

If you'd like to share what they posted:

- Most platforms (Facebook, Instagram, and others) let you **download everything** as a file you can send us
- If you'd like help doing that, we can walk you through it step by step
- If you'd rather skip this entirely, no problem at all

**If you shared an account with them, or aren't sure what's yours to share:** just ask. We'll figure it out together.

---

## A Note on Private or Sensitive Things

If you come across anything deeply personal — health information, private struggles, legal matters — you don't have to share it. We can build a beautiful reflection of them without it.

If you do choose to share something sensitive because it matters to who they were:

- It's kept especially safe — encrypted, restricted, never used for anything beyond bringing them closer to you
- It's removed from our system as soon as we've captured what we need
- It's never shown to anyone who isn't part of building this for you

You're the judge of what feels right. Trust yourself.

---

## When You're Ready (Or Not)

There is no deadline. There is no minimum. There is no "right amount."

Some people share everything in one afternoon. Others add a little at a time over weeks, or months, and discover things they'd forgotten along the way. Both are beautiful. Both work.

**When you have something to share, send it to Pat.** However it arrives — email, message, attachment, link, screenshot — is fine. If you're not sure how to send a particular thing, just ask.

**If you'd rather stop here,** that's okay too. Even just the questionnaire and a few memories can create something meaningful. The person you love is already in those words. We just help bring them forward.

---

## If You Have Any Questions

Nothing in this process is too small to ask about. If you're wondering about any of this — what to share, how to share it, whether something is okay — Pat is always a message away, and genuinely happy to help.

*Everything you share is an act of love. We'll treat it that way.*

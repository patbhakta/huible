What the machines can't hear yet.

The correlation map

Every cell is the signed phi correlation between what a human heard (rows) and what emotion2vec — the strongest open speech-emotion model — predicted (columns), across 320 human-annotated conversational clips. Gold cells are agreement. Blue is the model actively avoiding the right answer.

  • Neutral is the blind spot. Over half of calm, neutral speech is read as joy. A voice AI that can't recognize calm misreads every quiet conversation.
  • Disgust barely registers (+0.05) — it scatters into joy, sadness and neutral almost at random.
  • The big four hold. Joy (+0.43), surprise (+0.53), anger (+0.50), sadness (+0.47) — the model genuinely hears high-arousal emotions. Human texture lives in the classes it can't.

Where the public data runs dry

Our corpus target is 250–500 samples per emotion class. The basic seven are publicly covered many times over. The classes that make speech feel human — amusement, confusion, pain, interest, contempt, awe — have no public source at all. That gap is our collection lane.

Why we publish our failure analysis

Every paid emotion API we consider must beat this open baseline on our gold set before we spend a dollar on it. Publishing the map keeps us honest: the numbers above are the gate. And the empty half of the coverage chart is the roadmap — the emotions nobody has data for are the ones worth collecting.

Methodology: gold labels are human-annotated MELD majority votes (320/320 join verified) and Hume research-page sampler filenames (96 vocal bursts); model scored via emotion2vec_plus_large. Full methodology, manifest and per-sample predictions live in our EQ corpus vault.
